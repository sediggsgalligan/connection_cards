Why we made this

Common Ground came out of the first FutureWorks Lab pilot at SAP Labs U.S., where we designed and tested a range of workplace connection activities with early-career employees across the U.S.

Of everything we tried, self-disclosure activities worked best, both in person and virtually. These are structured prompts that invite people to share their own thoughts, feelings, and stories, which builds trust quickly. This game organizes those prompts around SAP’s four GROWTH behaviors.

What the research shows

In a classic study by Aron and colleagues (1997), strangers who spent 45 minutes on escalating self-disclosure questions reached roughly 82% of the closeness people feel toward their closest relationship.

Our pilot found a similar result: self-disclosure activities drove significantly more relationship growth than comparable task- and play-based activities, moving participants up about two levels on a relationship closeness scale on average, for example from stranger to friendly acquaintance, or from acquaintance to work friend.

Common Ground

Building Connections Through Conversation

These questions are adapted from Arthur Aron's "Fast Friends" procedure, a research-backed method for building interpersonal connection. We've reworked the format for the workplace, organized around SAP's four GROWTH behaviors.

The Four Behaviors

GROWTH at SAP

We Embrace Curiosity
Strive to get better every day with a passion for self-development.
We Take Charge
Take ownership of your actions, focusing on meaningful outcomes.
We Act with Transparency
Communicating openly, always acting with integrity and respect.
We Win as One
Harness collective capability to deliver winning results.
How To Play

Your hand is yours alone

1
Hold
You hold four questions, one from each GROWTH behavior, plus a twist card. Nobody else in your group holds the same questions.
2
Take turns
Go around by seat number. On your turn, pick a question from your hand, read it aloud and answer first.
3
Respond
Others can ask a follow-up, share their own answer, or say what part of your answer they connected with most.
4
Reflect
Play until everyone has taken three or four turns, or until time runs out, then wrap up with a short reflection.
Twist Cards

Shake up the conversation

Every two questions you lead earns you a twist card. Twists change how people share, never who wins. Keep yours hidden until the right moment.
